Flatline vs Healthchecks.io: who should run your dead man's switch?

By Auto Company · 5 min read

Healthchecks.io and Flatline solve the identical problem: a cron job or scheduled task fails silently — no error, no crash, no alert, it just stops running — and nobody notices until someone asks why last night's backup is missing. Both work the same way: your job pings a URL after every successful run, and if the ping doesn't show up inside a window you define, you get told. That's a dead man's switch, and the mechanism is the same on both sides.

What's different is who runs the thing doing the watching, and that one decision drives everything else — pricing, what alerting you get for free, and what your job's schedule and webhook URLs end up sitting in.

The question: do you want to operate this, or pay someone to?

Healthchecks.io is a hosted SaaS. You sign up, create a check, and you're pinging within a couple of minutes — nothing to deploy, nothing to maintain, and it's been running in production for other people for years. Flatline is open source and self-hosted: you deploy it to your own Cloudflare account (there's a one-click button for it), and from that point on it's entirely yours — your D1 database, your checks, no third party ever sees your job's cadence or your webhook endpoints.

Healthchecks.io: hosted, batteries-included alerting

Healthchecks.io's free Hobbyist tier is generous and has been stable for a long time: 20 checks, 100 log entries per check, no credit card, forever. Paid tiers scale from there — Business at $20/mo gets you 100 checks plus SMS and phone-call credits on top of the 25+ built-in notification channels (email, Slack, Discord, PagerDuty, webhooks, and more); Business Plus at $80/mo goes to 1,000 checks with more SMS/call credits.

The thing you're actually paying for past the free tier isn't really "more checks" — it's not having to wire up your own alerting. If you want a phone call at 3am when a job goes down, Healthchecks.io already has that relationship with a telephony provider so you don't have to build one.

Flatline: self-hosted, webhook-only, no per-check bill

Flatline ships with a free/pro/business tier model already built into the code (25 / 100 / 1,000 checks, with a minimum ping interval per tier), but since you're self-hosting, self-serve registration only ever grants the free tier — there's no billing to unlock the higher ones, they're just there in the schema if you want to grant yourself `pro` or `business` directly. In practice, that means whatever Cloudflare's free tier covers is what your monitoring costs: usually $0.

The trade-off is that Flatline's alerting is webhook-only — there's no built-in email or SMS relay, on purpose, because that's also a third-party integration surface Flatline doesn't want in its own request path. You point the webhook at whatever already accepts a POST: a Slack incoming webhook, a Discord webhook, PagerDuty's webhook intake, or your own endpoint. If you already have an alerting channel wired up for other things, this is one line to add, not a new provider relationship. If you don't, it's a bit more setup than Healthchecks.io's "just check the SMS box."

The real trade-off isn't price, it's custody

Both are cheap at small scale — Healthchecks.io's free tier and Flatline's self-hosted free tier both cover a solo developer's cron jobs without a card on file. The actual decision is about where your data and your ops burden live:

  • Pick Healthchecks.io if you want zero infrastructure to run, you want SMS or phone-call alerts without building a relay yourself, and you're fine with a third party holding your job schedules and alert destinations.
  • Pick Flatline if you'd rather your check definitions and webhook URLs never leave your own Cloudflare account, you already have a webhook-based alert channel (Slack/Discord/PagerDuty), and you don't mind that "self-hosted" means you clicked one deploy button and now it's your Worker.

Can you use both?

Yes — plenty of setups do. Use Healthchecks.io for the jobs where you genuinely want a phone call if something breaks, and self-host Flatline for internal or lower-stakes jobs where you'd rather not put another vendor between you and your own infrastructure, or where a Slack ping is already enough. They're not fighting for the same check; they're just answering "who operates this" differently.
